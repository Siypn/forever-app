Block settings
Set counts and RIR descend on a periodized schedule: weeks 1–3 hold base volume (2 sets compound / 3 isolation), weeks 4–5 are your overload peak (3/4 sets) — mesocycle research shows adding sets is the best-supported lever for hypertrophy — and week 6 is a deload, matching what most competitive strength/physique athletes already do.
Right now
The old "30-minute anabolic window" is outdated — research since (Aragon & Schoenfeld's nutrient-timing reviews) shows the practical window for protein/carbs after training is several hours wide, not a sharp cutoff, and total daily intake matters far more than exact timing. This still leans toward sooner rather than much later, especially if you trained without eating beforehand.
Today's targets
Cuts target a 0.5–1% bodyweight loss per week (~500 kcal/day deficit) to preserve muscle. Lean bulks target a 200–350 kcal/day surplus (~0.25–0.5 kg/week gain) — bigger surpluses mostly add fat past that point. Protein is set near 1g per pound of bodyweight. Fat sits at 25% of calories, carbs fill the remainder. Push weeks (4–5) nudge calories up; deload nudges down.

Bodyweight
BMI only relates height to total weight — it cannot tell muscle from fat. A lean, muscular lifter routinely scores "overweight" or "obese" on BMI despite low body fat. Treat the category label as a population-level screening tool, not a verdict on your physique.

Show Prep
This checks your timeline against the sustainable fat-loss rate from the sports-nutrition literature — 0.5–1% of bodyweight per week preserves muscle during a cut; going meaningfully faster raises the risk of losing muscle along with fat. It covers the diet-phase math only. Peak week (water, sodium, and carb manipulation in the final days) is a separate, highly individual process that benefits from an experienced coach — this tool won't tell you how to run it, and getting genuinely stage-lean for the first time is not something to navigate from an app alone.
Enter a show date and target weight to see your required pace.
Choose your split
Every option here trains each muscle at least twice a week — the one piece of programming the frequency research (Schoenfeld et al.) agrees on most consistently once volume is equal. Pick the one that fits your schedule and recovery, not the one that sounds hardest.
